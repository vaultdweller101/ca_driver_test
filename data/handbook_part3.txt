SECTION 7. Laws and Rules of the Road
Traffic Signals:
- Solid Red Light: STOP. You can turn right at a red light if there is not a NO TURN ON RED sign posted and you stop at the stop or limit line, yield for pedestrians, and turn when it is safe.
- Red Arrow: STOP. Do not turn at a red arrow. Remain stopped until a green traffic signal light or green arrow appears.
- Flashing Red Light: STOP. After stopping, you may go when it is safe.
- Solid Yellow Light: CAUTION. The light is about to turn red. Stop if you can do so safely.
- Yellow Arrow: The protected turning time is ending.
- Flashing Yellow Light: PROCEED WITH CAUTION. Slow down and be alert. You do not need to stop.
- Flashing Yellow Arrow: You can turn, but your turn is not protected from other traffic. Yield to oncoming traffic and proceed with caution.
- Solid Green Light: GO. Still stop for any vehicle, bicyclist, or pedestrian in the intersection. Do not enter the intersection if you cannot get completely across before the traffic signal light turns red.
- Green Arrow: GO in the direction the arrow is pointing (protected turn; oncoming vehicles are stopped by a red traffic signal light).
- Traffic Light Not Working: Stop as if the intersection is controlled by STOP signs in all directions.
Pedestrian Signals or Signs:
- WALK or Walking Person: You may cross the street.
- DON'T WALK or Raised Hand: You may not cross the street.
- Flashing DON'T WALK or Flashing Raised Hand: Do not start crossing the street. Drivers must yield to pedestrians, even if the DON'T WALK light is flashing.
- Diagonal Crossing: Crisscross and diagonal crosswalks that allow pedestrians to cross the intersection in any direction at the same time when the WALK signal allows it.
Signs:
- STOP Sign: 8-sided red sign. Make a full stop before entering the crosswalk or at the limit line.
- Red YIELD Sign: 3-sided downward triangle. Slow down and be ready to stop to let any vehicle, bicyclist, or pedestrian pass before you proceed.
- Red and White Regulatory Signs: Follow the sign's instruction (e.g., DO NOT ENTER, WRONG WAY, No U-Turn, No Left Turn, No Right Turn). If you are driving at night, you will know you are going the wrong way if the road reflectors shine red in your headlights.
- Yellow and Black Circular Sign or X-shaped Sign (R R): You are approaching a railroad crossing.
- 5-sided Sign (Pentagon): You are near a school. Drive slowly and stop for children in the crosswalk.
- Diamond-shaped Sign (Yellow or Orange): Warns you of specific road conditions and dangers ahead (Yellow = warning signs; Orange = highway construction and maintenance signs).
- White Rectangular Sign: Communicates many important rules you must obey (regulatory).
- Guide Signs: Green (directional/highway guidance), Blue ( motorist services / rest areas), Brown (recreational/cultural interest).
- Slow Moving Vehicle: Orange and red triangle on the back of vehicles traveling 25 mph or less.
Right-of-Way Rules: Who Goes First?
- Without STOP or YIELD signs: The vehicle that arrives to the intersection first has the right-of-way. However, if a vehicle, pedestrian, or bicyclist gets to the intersection at the same time as you, give the right-of-way to the vehicle, pedestrian, or bicyclist on your right.
- T intersections without STOP or YIELD signs: Vehicles, bicyclists, and pedestrians on the through road (continuing to go straight) have the right-of-way.
- Turning left: Give the right-of-way to any pedestrian or approaching vehicle that is close enough to be dangerous.
- Entering traffic: Yield to traffic already occupying the lanes. It is against the law to stop or block an intersection where there is not enough space to completely cross before the traffic signal light turns red.
- Roundabouts: Traffic travels in a counterclockwise direction around a central island. Yield to all traffic already in the roundabout. Enter heading to the right when there is a big enough gap. Do not stop or pass in a roundabout. Signal when you change lanes or exit. If you miss your exit, continue around until you return to your exit.
- Pedestrians: Includes a person walking, a person traveling on something other than a vehicle or bicycle (roller skates, skateboard, etc.), or a person with a disability using a tricycle, quadricycle, or wheelchair. Do not pass a vehicle stopped at a crosswalk. If a pedestrian makes eye contact with you, they are ready to cross the street; yield to the pedestrian. School crossings may have yellow crosswalk lines.
- Pedestrians Who Are Blind: Pedestrians using guide dogs or white canes have the right-of-way at all times. Do not stop in the middle of a crosswalk. Do not honk your horn at a blind person. When a blind person pulls in their cane and steps away from the intersection, this gesture usually means you may go.
- Mountain Roads: If two vehicles meet on a steep narrow road and neither vehicle can pass, the vehicle facing uphill has the right-of-way. The vehicle facing downhill has more control when backing up the hill and should back up until the vehicle going uphill can pass.
Sharing the Road:
- Large Vehicles / Trucks:
  * Blind Spots (the No Zone): If you cannot see the truck's side mirrors, the truck driver cannot see you.
  * Braking: Average passenger vehicle at 55 mph can stop within 300 feet; a large vehicle at 55 mph can take up to 400 feet to stop.
  * Turning: Rear wheels follow a shorter path than front wheels, so trucks often swing wide.
  * Maneuvering: On a divided highway with four or more traffic lanes in one direction, large vehicles/trucks may be driven in the two lanes farthest to the right.
- Buses, Streetcars, Trolleys:
  * Safety zones are spaces set aside for pedestrians waiting for buses, streetcars, and trolleys, marked by raised buttons or markers (or dotted white lines) on a road. Do not drive through a safety zone under any condition.
  * When a bus, streetcar, or trolley is stopped at a safety zone or traffic light, you may pass at no more than 10 mph.
  * Do not overtake and pass a light rail vehicle or streetcar on the left side, whether it is moving or standing, unless the tracks are so close to the right side that the passenger vehicle cannot pass on the right, you are on a one-way street, or a traffic officer directs you to pass on the left.
- Motorcycles:
  * Allow a safe three-second following distance.
  * It is legal to share lanes with motorcycles; this is known as lane splitting.
  * Never try to pass a motorcycle in the same lane as you.
- Emergency Vehicles:
  * Drive to the right edge of the road and stop until the emergency vehicle(s) using a siren and red lights have passed.
  * If you are in an intersection when you see an emergency vehicle, continue through the intersection, then drive to the right as soon as it is safe and stop.
  * It is against the law to follow within 300 feet of any fire engine, law enforcement vehicle, ambulance, or other emergency vehicle when their siren or flashing lights are on.
- Slow-moving Vehicles:
  * NEVs (Neighborhood Electric Vehicles) and LSVs (Low-speed Vehicles) reach a maximum speed of 25 mph. They are restricted from roads where the speed limit is greater than 35 mph.
- Animal-drawn Vehicles: It is against the law to intentionally scare horses or livestock.
- Bicycles:
  * Bicyclists under 18 years old must wear a helmet.
  * Bicycling at Night requires:
    - A front lamp with a white light visible from 300 feet.
    - A built-in rear red reflector, solid red light, or flashing red light visible from 500 feet.
    - A white or yellow reflector on each pedal, the bicyclist's shoes, or their ankles visible from 200 feet.
    - A white or yellow reflector on the front wheel, a white or red reflector on the rear wheel, or reflectorized tires.
  * Passing a Bicyclist: Allow at least three (3) feet between your vehicle and the bicyclist. Enter a bike lane no more than 200 feet before starting a turn.
- Road Workers and Work Zones:
  * Fines for traffic violations in a work zone can be $1,000 or more. Anyone convicted of assaulting a highway worker faces fines of up to $2,000 and imprisonment for up to one year.
  * Fines are doubled in Safety Enhanced-Double Fine Zones and in highway construction or maintenance zones when workers are present.
  * Move Over and Slow Down for stationary emergency vehicles, tow trucks, or stopped road work vehicles displaying flashing amber warning lights.
- Vehicles with Hazardous Loads: Must stop before crossing railroad tracks.
- Towing: Must drive in the far-right lane or a lane marked for slower vehicles when you tow a vehicle or trailer, or drive a truck with three or more axles. (If four or more lanes in your direction, you may drive in the two lanes closest to the right edge of the road.)
- Speed Limits in Special Zones:
  * Around Children / School Zones: The speed limit is 25 mph within 500 feet of a school while children are outside or crossing the street (some school zones may have speed limits as low as 15 mph).
  * Stopped School Buses: When a school bus flashes red lights, you must stop from either direction until the children are safely across the street and the lights stop flashing. Failure to stop can result in a fine of up to $1,000 and driving privilege suspended for one year. If the school bus is on the other side of a divided or multilane highway (two or more lanes in each direction), you do not need to stop.
  * Blind Intersections: Speed limit is 15 mph if within 100 feet of an intersection where you cannot see for 100 feet in both directions and there are no stop signs at any corner.
  * Alleys: Any road no wider than 25 feet used to access rear/side entrances. The speed limit in an alley is 15 mph.
  * Near Railroad or Light Rail Tracks: The speed limit is 15 mph within 100 feet of a railroad crossing when you cannot see the tracks for 400 feet in both directions (unless controlled by gates, a warning signal, or a flagman). When crossing devices or a person warns you a train is coming, stop at least 15 feet from the nearest track.
  * Business or Residential Districts: The speed limit is 25 mph, unless otherwise posted.
Other Important Roadway Information:
- Do not smoke when a minor is in the vehicle.
- Do not dump or abandon animals on a highway (punishable by a fine of up to $1,000, six months in jail, or both).
- Do not carry anything in or on a passenger vehicle which extends beyond the fenders on the left side or more than 6 inches beyond the fenders on the right side.
- Cargo that extends more than 4 feet from the back-rear bumper of the vehicle must display a 12-inch red or fluorescent orange square flag (at night, marked with two red lights).
- Do not transport animals in the back of a pickup or other truck unless the animal is properly secured.
- Objects on windshield/windows may only be affixed in:
  * A 7-inch square on the lower corner of the passenger's side windshield or the lower corner of the rear window.
  * A 5-inch square on the lower corner of the driver's side window.
  * A 5-inch square located in the center uppermost portion of your windshield for an electronic toll payment device.
  * On the side windows behind the driver.
- Do not interfere with a funeral procession (led by a traffic officer and has the right-of-way).
Evading Law Enforcement:
- Misdemeanor to use a motor vehicle to flee or attempt to evade law enforcement (punishable by imprisonment in a county jail for one year or less).
- Causing serious bodily injury during a pursuit: state prison for up to 7 years, or county jail for 1 year or less.
- Manslaughter resulting from evading law enforcement during a pursuit: state prison for a minimum of 4 to 10 years.
- If an unlicensed person is caught driving your vehicle, it may be impounded for 30 days.
Points on Your Driver's Record & Negligent Operator:
- Traffic convictions and collisions stay on your record for 36 months or longer.
- As an adult, your license may be suspended if your driver's record shows one of the following point totals:
  * 4 points in 12 months.
  * 6 points in 24 months.
  * 8 points in 36 months.
- Traffic Violator School: For a one-point traffic violation, you may attend once in any 18-month period.
- DMV will revoke your driving privilege if you are convicted of a hit-and-run or reckless driving that resulted in injury.
Maintaining Your Minor's (Provisional) Driver's License:
- Two at-fault collisions, two traffic violation convictions, or one of each: You cannot drive for 30 days unless a licensed adult, at least 25 years old, rides with you.
- Three at-fault collisions, three traffic violation convictions, or a combination: Your driving privilege will be suspended for six months and you will be on probation for one year.
- Administrative Hearing: You must request a hearing within 10 days of being served or 14 days from the date the notice is mailed.
- Record Confidentiality: Most information in your driver's record is available to the public, except physical or mental conditions, address, and social security number.
