SECTION 8. Safe Driving
Be Aware of Your Surroundings:
- Scan Your Surroundings: Always keep your eyes moving and scan the road at least 10 seconds ahead of your vehicle.
- Tailgating (Following Too Closely): Use the three-second rule to ensure a safe following distance and avoid a collision. If a tailgater is behind you, maintain your course and speed; then, when safe to do so, merge right to change into another lane and allow the tailgater to pass.
- Blind Spots: To check your blind spots, look over your right and left shoulders out of your side windows. Only turn your head when you look.
Understand the Road Conditions:
- Darkness: Use high-beam headlights on open country roads or dark city streets. If another vehicle's lights are too bright, do not look directly into the oncoming headlights; look toward the right edge of your lane and watch the oncoming vehicle out of the corner of your eye. When a vehicle with one light drives toward you, drive as far to the right as possible.
- Sun Glare: Wear polarized sunglasses, keep inside and outside of windshield clean.
- Skids:
  * Slippery Surface Skids: If you start to skid: 1. Slowly remove your foot from the accelerator. 2. Do not use the brakes. 3. Turn the steering wheel in the direction of the skid. 4. Try to get a wheel on dry pavement.
  * Locked Wheel Skids: With four-wheel ABS, apply firm pressure on the brake pedal. Without ABS, quickly pump your brakes.
  * Wet brakes: You can dry them by lightly pressing the accelerator and brake pedals at the same time until dry.
- Slippery Roads: Adjust your speed for different conditions:
  * Wet road: Reduce your speed by 5 to 10 mph.
  * Packed snow: Reduce your speed by half.
  * Ice: Reduce your speed to no more than 5 mph.
  * Bridges and overpasses tend to freeze before the rest of the road.
  * When it starts to rain, the pavement can be very slippery (especially if it has been dry and hot for some time, because oil and dust have not been washed away).
  * In a heavy rainstorm or snowstorm, if you cannot see farther than 100 feet, it is not safe to drive faster than 30 mph.
- Hydroplaning: Occurs when a vehicle rides on water because the tires lose all contact with the road. If your vehicle starts to hydroplane, slow down gradually and do not use the brakes.
- High Winds: Reduce your speed, maintain a firm hand position on the steering wheel, and do not use cruise control.
- Fog or Heavy Smoke: Drive slowly and use your low-beam headlights (high-beam headlights will reflect back and cause glare). Never drive using only your parking or fog lights.
- Law Enforcement Traffic Breaks: The officer turns on their emergency lights and slowly weaves across lanes. Turn on your emergency flashers, slowly decrease your speed to the same speed as the officer, and do not drive past the patrol vehicle.
Protect Yourself and Your Passengers:
- Seat Belts: You and your passengers must wear seat belts. If your passenger is under 16 years old, you can also get a ticket if they are not wearing their seat belt. If you are pregnant, wear the lap belt as low as possible under your abdomen.
- Child Restraint System and Safety Seats:
  * Children under 2 years old, under 40 pounds, and under 3 feet 4 inches tall: Secure in a rear-facing child passenger restraint system. A child in a rear-facing child passenger restraint system may not ride in the front seat of an airbag-equipped vehicle.
  * Children under 8 years old, or who are less than 4 feet 9 inches tall: Secure in a child passenger restraint system in a rear seat.
  * Children who are 8 years old or older, or at least 4 feet 9 inches tall: May use a properly secured safety belt that meets federal standards.
- Air Bags: Ride at least 10 inches from the airbag cover (measured from the center of the steering wheel to your breastbone).
- Unattended Children and Pets: It is illegal to leave a child who is six (6) years old or younger unattended in a vehicle. A child may be left under the supervision of a person who is at least 12 years old. It is dangerous and illegal to leave children or animals in a hot vehicle.
Manage Your Speed:
- Basic Speed Law: In California, you may never drive faster than is safe for the current road conditions.
- Unless otherwise posted, the ideal maximum speed limit on most California highways is 65 mph.
- It is 55 mph on a two-lane undivided highway and for vehicles towing trailers.
Know How to Handle Emergencies:
- Tire Blowouts: Hold the steering wheel with both hands, maintain your vehicle speed if possible and safe, gradually release the accelerator, correct the steering to stabilize, and pull off the road when safe.
- Driving Off the Pavement: Grip the steering wheel firmly, remove your foot from the accelerator, brake gently, check for traffic behind you, and carefully steer back onto the pavement.
- Overheating Conditions: In extreme heat, watch the temperature gauge, avoid driving at high speeds for long periods, and turn off the air conditioner.
- Disabled Vehicles on Railroad Tracks:
  * If a train is approaching and warning lights are flashing: Immediately exit your vehicle and run away from the tracks diagonally in the direction the train is coming from. Then call 911.
  * If no train is approaching: Exit your vehicle, dial the number located on the railroad crossing posts or metal control box near the tracks, provide the crossing number, then call 911.
Do Not Drive Distracted:
- Adult drivers should only use a cell phone in hands-free mode when necessary. Mount the cell phone on the windshield, dashboard, or center console (cannot block view) and use a single swipe or touch feature.
- Minors and Cell Phones: It is against the law for a minor to use a cell phone or electronic wireless device to answer calls and send or respond to text messages while driving (even hands-free), EXCEPT to make a call for emergency assistance.
- Carbon Monoxide: Deadly odorless gas released from a vehicle's exhaust pipe. Never start your vehicle inside the garage with the door closed.

SECTION 9. Alcohol and Drugs
- If a law enforcement officer thinks you are driving while under the influence of drugs or alcohol, they have the right to ask you to take a blood or urine test. If you refuse to take one, DMV will suspend or revoke your driving privilege for one year.
- If you are 13 to 20 years old and convicted of operating a bicycle while under the influence of alcohol or drugs, your driving privilege may be suspended or delayed for one year once you are eligible to drive.
- Use or Possession of Alcohol or Cannabis Products in a Vehicle: Containers must be sealed and unopened, or kept in the trunk or a place where passengers do not sit. It is illegal to keep an open container of alcohol in your glove box. (Does not apply if you are a passenger in a bus, taxi, camper, or motorhome.)
- Blood Alcohol Concentration (BAC) Limits — It is illegal for you to drive if you have a BAC of:
  * 0.08% or higher if you are 21 years old or older.
  * 0.01% or higher if you are under 21 years old.
  * 0.01% or higher at any age if you are on DUI probation.
  * 0.04% or higher if you drive a vehicle that requires a commercial driver's license.
  * 0.04% or higher if you are driving a passenger for hire.
- DUI Arrests & Convictions:
  * Under Administrative Per Se law, the officer may take your driver's license and give you a temporary driver's license for 30 days. You may request a DMV administrative hearing within 10 days from the date of your arrest.
  * All DUI convictions remain on your driver's record for 10 years.
- Drivers Under 21:
  * If caught with alcohol in your vehicle, law enforcement can impound your vehicle for up to 30 days, fine you, and suspend your driver's license for one year.

SECTION 10. Financial Responsibility, Insurance Requirements, and Collisions
- Insurance Requirements — Your insurance must cover at least:
  * $30,000 for a single death or injury.
  * $60,000 for death or injury to more than one person.
  * $15,000 for property damage.
- Driving Without Insurance: Your driving privilege will be suspended for up to four (4) years if you are in a collision and do not have proper insurance coverage, regardless of who was at fault.
- What to Do if You Are in a Collision:
  * You must stop (failing to stop is hit-and-run).
  * You must make a report to law enforcement within 24 hours of the collision if anyone is injured or killed.
  * If you kill or injure an animal, call the nearest humane society or law enforcement. Do not try to move an injured animal.
- Reporting a Collision to DMV:
  * If you are in a collision, you must report it to DMV within 10 days (using form SR 1) if the collision caused more than $1,000 in damage to property, or if anyone was injured or killed (even minor injuries). This applies even on private property and regardless of fault.

SECTION 11. Vehicle Registration Requirements
- Buying or Selling a Vehicle:
  * When you buy a vehicle, you have 10 days to transfer ownership to your name.
  * When you sell a vehicle, you must notify DMV within 5 days by completing a Notice of Transfer and Release of Liability.
- Out-of-State Vehicles:
  * You have 20 days to register your vehicle after you become a resident or get a job in California.

SECTION 12. Driver Safety
- Reexamination: A physical or mental condition or poor driver's record can be the basis for a reexamination, not a driver's age.
- Priority Reexamination: If you receive a Notice of Priority Reexamination of Driver from law enforcement, you have five (5) working days to contact DMV to initiate the process or your driving privilege will be automatically suspended.
- Note: There are no specific restrictions for seniors. All restrictions are based on conditions, not age.

SECTION 13. Seniors and Driving
- Warning signs of an unsafe driver: Getting lost in familiar places, dents and scrapes on the car/fences/mailbox/garage doors, frequent close calls or collisions.
- Driver's License Renewal: If you are 70 years old or older at the time your driver's license expires, you are required to renew your driver's license in person and pass a vision test. DMV sends a renewal notice about 90 days before expiration.
- Mature Driver Program: An eight-hour course for drivers 55 years old and older. Certificate is valid for three years and can be renewed by completing another four-hour course.
- Senior ID Cards: If you are 62 years old or older, you are eligible for a no-fee Senior ID card (or drivers of any age who are unable to continue driving safely due to a physical or mental condition).

SECTION 14. Glossary
- Behind-the-wheel drive test: A drive test where you have control of the vehicle and are accompanied by a DMV examiner who is evaluating your driving skills.
- Blood alcohol concentration (BAC): The amount of alcohol in your blood (e.g., 0.10% means 0.10 grams of alcohol in 100 milliliters of blood).
- Driving performance evaluation (DPE): The portion of the behind-the-wheel drive test when you drive your car with a DMV examiner who evaluates your driving skills.
- Pedestrian: A person walking and can also be a person with a disability using a tricycle, quadricycle, or wheelchair for transportation.
- Right-of-way: Helps determine who is allowed to go first in situations where vehicles, pedestrians, and cyclists meet on the road.
- Three-second rule: A driving rule that helps you estimate how closely you should follow other vehicles. When the vehicle in front of you passes a certain point, count three seconds. If you pass the same point before you finish counting, you are following too closely.
- Traffic citation: Also known as a ticket, an official summons issued by law enforcement for violating a traffic law.
- Vulnerable road users (VRU): Non-motorized road users, such as cyclists, pedestrians, and persons with disabilities or reduced mobility and orientation using a wheelchair, tricycle, or quadricycle.
- Yield: Means to wait for, slow down, and be ready to stop (if necessary) to allow other vehicles or pedestrians who have right-of-way to proceed.
